validation, correlation, luck removal

Strategy lab: gates first, leaderboard later

A leaderboard is only worth looking at if a strategy has to earn its place. This page defines the test first and the ranking second — eight gates, a correlation-aware pricing method, and a luck-removal protocol. No strategy has passed anything yet, because no bet has been settled.

The leaderboard is empty on purpose. A leaderboard row requires settled, graded bets. Zero bets have been settled in this repo, so no strategy has a record.

A strategy must pass every gate before a leaderboard row is shown. Gates are pre-registered so the thresholds cannot be chosen after seeing results.

Correlation — the parlay problem

Multiplying leg probabilities assumes independence. For same-game legs that assumption is wrong, and it is wrong in the direction that makes a parlay look better than it is. The engine prices two-leg parlays with a Gaussian copula and refuses to price at all when the correlation has not been estimated.

Two legs at 55% each. At ρ = 0.6 the true joint probability is 40.4%, not 30.3% — a parlay that looks like a 3.3x payout is really worth about 2.5x. This is exactly the error gate G7 exists to prevent.

Design only. The scheme allocates no money and has no verified historical returns behind it.

Ranking metric: risk-adjusted: ROI / stdev of ROI, then deflated by the number of strategies tried (L5). Raw ROI is explicitly not the ranking metric.. Raw ROI is deliberately not the ranking metric, because it rewards the luckiest high-variance strategy.

What would fill the leaderboard

  • A settled-bet log with strategy id, market, price taken, closing price, stake and result.
  • At least 200 settled bets per strategy before a row is displayed (gate G1).
  • All eight gates recorded pass/fail with linked evidence.

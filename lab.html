Two results, one of them unwelcome

The brief asks for injury effects as an ablation on chronological holdouts rather than a fixed point penalty, and for a leaderboard ranked by a proper score with a cluster lower bound and a multiplicity adjustment, with a demotion gate to paper. Both are built. Neither flatters the model, and the pages says so.

1 · Injury ablation

Model M1 adds a personnel term for depth-1 players the official report had already ruled out; the coefficient is fitted on the training slice only. Model M0 is the same model without it. They are compared on the same games, so the interval is paired.

All three intervals cross zero. On the only week where a depth chart exists, adding personnel information made the model slightly worse on every metric, and the difference is inside the noise of thirteen games. The honest conclusion is that this repository cannot yet demonstrate that an injury term helps — which is exactly the reason the brief forbids a fixed penalty. A fixed penalty of, say, 2 points per missing starter would have produced the same forty-point error bars while looking authoritative.

Thirteen games is not enough to settle it either way. This is reported as insufficient evidence, not as evidence of absence, and the coefficient is published so the next week can move it.

2 · Leaderboard v2 — proper score, not ROI

RETROSPECTIVE BACKTEST — not the prospective leaderboard. Every number in this table is scored on games already played (Weeks 5–10, in-sample). By rule R010 these rows are not eligible for the prospective leaderboard. Machine-readable: dataset_type=RETROSPECTIVE_BACKTEST, prospective=false on every row (data/lab/leaderboard_v2.json). Guard: scripts/check_leaderboard_governance.py.

85 games, nine strategies scored on the same fixtures. Skill is 1 − Brier ÷ Brier(coin). The interval is a game-cluster bootstrap. The p-value is Holm-adjusted across the five non-baseline strategies. A strategy clears the gate only if its adjusted p is below 0.05 and its 2.5% cluster bound is above zero.

Nothing clears. The best row is FL3 ratings, no home edge at skill +0.131, but its 2.5% bound is −0.037 and its Holm-adjusted p is 0.21. Under the pre-registered rule that is a demotion to paper, and the table says so in the same row as the encouraging number.

Note what the baselines establish: "always home" is catastrophically bad (skill −1.35) and "always away" is bad (−0.65). Home advantage is real; the question the leaderboard is testing is whether this model adds anything beyond knowing who is at home. Marginally, it does not yet.

What would change our mind — for each strategy

Every row in data/lab/leaderboard_v2.json carries a what_would_change_our_mind and a what_would_falsify_it list. In summary, for all of them: a second season's games the strategy was not fitted on, and the same sign of skill under a different proper score (log loss), would move a row out of paper. A bootstrap interval that still includes zero, or skill that flips sign between Weeks 5-7 and Weeks 8-10, would kill it.

A calibration note, stated because it is uncomfortable. The rating differential is overconfident: the best fixed shrink of its probability toward 0.5 was 0.65, which lifts its Brier from 0.234 to 0.228. The leaderboard does not silently apply that shrink — that would be fitting on the test set. It is recorded here as the most promising next iteration, to be pre-registered before it is scored.

What is deliberately missing

  • ROI. Blank for every strategy, by policy PD-01. A rank by season-long ROI over 85 games would be a rank by luck; the brief rejects it and so does the code.
  • Win rate. Not a column. It drops ties and ignores confidence.
  • A three-way train/validation/holdout split. One season cannot supply one without leakage. The rule is declared in the results feed for the moment a second season exists and is explicitly not claimed as evidence today.

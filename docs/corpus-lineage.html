Flagged for Review — what does “136-game log” mean?

FLAGGED FOR REVIEW

The committed schedule transcript has 136 rows across Weeks 1–9, and the earlier comparison reconciliation labels that slice as matching the brief's “136-game log”. The later results-feed metadata says: The brief asks to 'turn the 136-game log into the pre-registered backtest corpus'. The verified count is not 136. It is 149 after adding the one missing official game to the 148 already transcribed. 136 is not reproducible from any official page and is not used anywhere. This is recorded as a corrected number, not as a discrepancy that blocks work.

Those statements conflict if the latter is read as saying that 136 cannot be reproduced at all. The saved rows establish 136 as a Weeks 1–9 slice; they do not establish whether that is the exact historical file or time window the brief intended. Do not treat 136 as the full Week 10 count or silently substitute 148/149.

Decision needed: identify the intended 136-game source/window, or explicitly register the dated 148/149 snapshots as separate corpora. Until then, retain each version and its timestamps.

What the comparison does and does not score

  • The retrospective comparison ledger has 148 rows and is labelled retrospective. Its score metrics must be read as the saved 148-row version, not as results computed on the later corrected feed.
  • The Week 10 PIT@CIN correction is not in that old ledger. This report preserves the existing scores rather than silently adding a game after evaluation.
  • The official results-feed metadata records LAC@BAL as excluded at the 2026-11-17 capture because the game was not final then. The current official final score is intentionally blank here until a later source read is captured and hashed.
  • Any new evaluation should be a separately versioned run: freeze its corpus, preserve its source timestamps/hashes, use chronological holdouts, and publish an explicit n for each metric.
Next clearing evidence. Re-read the official LAC@BAL game centre and, where available, the NFL Game Book; capture a timestamp and raw-file hash; then add a new results-feed version. Only after that should a new, separately identified evaluation be generated. Until then the current-score field stays blank and no present-day final count is asserted.

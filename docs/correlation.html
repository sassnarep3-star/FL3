Correlation - measurements, policy and the parlay math

The brief asks to "determine correlation values". Two honest halves: what we can measure from verified data right now, and the policy that decides how a correlated bet is priced and staked. The second half matters more in practice, because a wrong correlation number is at least checkable while a missing correlation policy is how a "winning" parlay system turns into a drawdown.

Read the intervals, not the point estimates:

  • Same-game home points vs away points: r = 0.12 (CI −0.23 to +0.44). The correlation that determines

the price of a two-team-total parlay is not distinguishable from anything between −0.2 and +0.4 on 33 games. Anyone quoting "0.1" as if it were a constant is quoting noise.

  • Off pass attempts vs rush attempts: r = −0.10 (CI −0.43 to +0.26). The "script effect" substitution

we assume inside a backfield is not yet visible at team level.

  • Net points vs pass yards: r = 0.46 (CI 0.14 to 0.70) vs **net points vs rush yards: r = 0.17

(CI −0.19 to 0.49).** Passing volume separates teams this season; rushing volume does not. That is a usable finding for how to weight features - and it is a 32-team cross-section, not a causal claim.

  • Pass yards allowed vs points allowed: r = 0.42 (CI 0.08 to 0.67) - the defensive-quality proxy

signals SIG-04/SIG-05 depend on has real content, weakly.

  • Sacks taken vs sacks generated by the same defence: r = −0.04. Nearly zero, as expected: an

O-line being bad and a pass rush being good are different facts. A model that treats "they get sacked" as "they won't get sacked" double-counts.

The mechanics those rows encode:


book's combined price   = product of the decimals it offers per leg
                        = (1 / implied_prob_per_leg) ^ k          # k = legs
true joint probability  = P(A and B)  !=  P(A) * P(B)  when A and B share a cause
with a copula-free view = p1*p2 + rho * sqrt(p1*q1*p2*q2)

On the computed example (two legs at −110, a 2-point hit-rate edge each): the ticket is worth +7.8% if the legs are independent, +39% if rho = +0.35 (which is roughly the correlation between a team total over and that team's QB passing yards over), and −23.9% if rho = −0.35. Same two bets, same prices, wildly different value - and the sign of the difference is decided entirely by the correlation we have not yet measured. That is the whole argument for why correlation is a first-class object in this repo rather than a footnote.

Policy (what the system does about it)

RuleStatementEnforcement
P-1Never price a multi-leg bet as independent. Use the joint from the game simulation, or do not offer it.gate G-07 blocks the strategy
P-2Two signals that fire on the same underlying fact (e.g. one QB Out producing three "different" angles) are one position, not three.gate G-08, dedupe key (game, causal driver)
P-3Correlation must be measured on the same distribution the bet is settled on (points, not outcomes) - a 20-pt margin and a 3-pt margin have different prop outcomesscripts/model.py measures on game rows, not win flags
P-4Diversification across games is a variance choice, not an edge. A 4-leg parlay of independent −110 legs multiplies the vig by 4 and the edge by 4 too.[decision math](data/decision_math.csv) parlay rows
P-5Same-game legs whose correlation the book cannot compute better than we can are where "extra" parlay value exists - and books restrict or re-price exactly those combinationsoperational: [Q-10](questions.html), [Q-11](questions.html)
P-6Correlation inputs carry the same verification badge as prices: measured-provisional may not set a stake, only a direction of travelbuild-time status column

What would let us put real numbers in the blanks

1. 5 seasons of settled games with lines (Q-01 + Q-04) → measure rho for every leg-pair we actually bet, with the sample size printed next to it. 2. Player-level rates (Q-08) → prop-vs-team correlations, the ones that decide whether a "stack" is really a single bet. 3. Book rules text (Q-11) → which combinations are restricted, and what the correlation adjustment they apply is; without it we can price a parlay but not know whether it is offered at that price.

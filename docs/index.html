FL3 - NFL line origination and signal board

A working system for originating NFL lines, turning them into betting signals, and grading the strategies that produce them - built only from data that can be traced to an official source, with every unconfirmable value left blank and listed on the [Flagged for review](flagged.html) page.

The site is generated. Every table you see is rendered from data/*.csv by scripts/build_site.py; if a value is not in the CSV it does not exist on the site, and if a CSV row fails an identity check the build stops. Start with the [Executive summary](executive-summary.html), then decide the items in the [Suggestion register](suggestions.html).

What is real right now

  • 32 of 32 clubs loaded from the official [standings](standings.html) page, identity-checked

(sum(PF) = sum(PA) = 6,291, sum(W) = sum(L) = 136).

  • 33 row-verified games of the 137 games the league has actually played through Week 9

([game results](games.html)) - the rest is a coverage gap we declare, not a gap we paper over.

  • 160 availability rows for the Week 10 slate, read off the [official injury report](injuries.html),

including the backup-vs-starter damage per position group.

  • A priced Week 10 slate with fitted constants - see [Lines](lines.html). The market columns are

blank on purpose: no official source publishes odds, so nothing was filled in by hand.

  • 15 signals, 13 validation gates, 20 open questions, 25 suggestions - each with a stated

dependency, so you can see what has to be bought or read before a decision is defensible.

The one-line status

The architecture, the data discipline and the evaluation machinery are done and reproducible. The pricing itself is not trustworthy yet, and it is not presented as such: the four numbers that make a spread into a price (home-field value, residual SD, shrinkage, vig) are fitted on 33 games and are labelled provisional-in-sample everywhere they appear.

Read it in this order

1. [Executive summary](executive-summary.html) - findings, not features. 2. [Questions & gaps](questions.html) - what must be answered before any decision. 3. [Suggestions](suggestions.html) - the paths, with impact/effort/risk; approve the ones you want. 4. [Model](model.html) → [Lines](lines.html) → [Signals](signals.html) → [Correlation](correlation.html) → [Validation gates](validation.html) → [Leaderboard & rewards](leaderboard.html). 5. [Methodology](methodology.html) and [Verification log](verification.html) to audit the numbers, and [Sources](sources.html) to click through to the official page behind any cell.

Nothing on this site is betting advice, and nothing here says a strategy is profitable. The best
claim the current data supports is: *this pipeline would detect it, and this is the evidence it
still needs.*

Data freshness — the snapshot is two days old and one round stale

Every page on this site is stamped "official data read 2026-11-14 UTC." Today is 2026-11-16. Week 10's Sunday slate was played on 2026-11-15.

That is not a cosmetic date problem. It means the site, as shipped, prices games that have already finished using rosters and injury designations that changed after they kicked off. This page measures the gap instead of describing it in the abstract.

The repository already held a transcription of the Week 10 injury report, read on 2026-11-14. This session made a second, independent transcription on 2026-11-16. Comparing them is the closest thing to a controlled experiment this repo has produced, and the result is the single most important thing learned about the data supply:

The same URL, read two days apart, produces materially different availability for the games that had not yet kicked off.

The pattern is not random and it is not a transcription error:

  • LAC carried a game status on 0 of 19 rows on 2026-11-14 and on 9 of 19 rows on 2026-11-16.

That is correct both times. A Monday club's final practice had not happened when the first read was taken, so the NFL had published no game designation yet.

  • Practice grades moved the ordinary way: Awosika, Njoku, Mitchell and Shelley went Limited →

Full; Pipkins went Limited → DNP.

  • BAL gained two rows (Calais Campbell, Ronnie Stanley) and two designations (Jaylinn

Hawkins Out, Adam Randall Questionable) between the reads.

  • IND Arden Key came off the report entirely — he was Hamstring/Questionable, now no injury and

no designation.

Both games affected — LAC at BAL and PIT at CIN — are the two games that had not been played.

Why this changes how the whole site must be read

A single capture is not a snapshot of the truth. It is a snapshot of a clock. Freezing one and pricing from it does not merely risk staleness; it prices LAC at BAL as though nine Chargers game designations did not exist, and it prices the Ravens' availability from a chart and a report taken before their final practice of the week.

That is lookahead bias running in reverse: not using future information, but using less information than was available and presenting it as the state of the world.

Two data-hygiene bugs found while measuring this

BugEffect
Rams abbreviation. data/injuries_2026_wk10.csv uses LAR; data/lines_week10.json and this session's capture use LA.A join on club abbreviation silently drops all 14 Rams availability rows. No error, no warning — just a team with no injuries.
Player name. The page's visible text is Jacob Hummel; the link it publishes is /players/jake-hummel/.NFL.com's own inconsistency. Recorded as published and flagged; not silently "corrected".

What was done, and what was deliberately not done

Done: a dated, hashed re-capture exists alongside the old one (data/captures/), the delta is published, and the two transcriptions are compared row by row rather than one overwriting the other.

Not done: the 2026-11-14 snapshot was not overwritten. It is the only pre-kickoff record in the repository, and pre-kickoff records are the entire basis of a backtest. Overwriting it to make a date look better would destroy the evidence needed to score the forecast. Superseding it is a decision with gate consequences, not a side effect of a measurement.

The requirement this evidences

Per the brief: "Treat the injury report as a timestamped series, not a weekly snapshot." This page is the proof of why that requirement exists, measured rather than argued.

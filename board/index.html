FL3 - NFL line origination and signal board

A working system for originating NFL lines, turning them into betting signals, and grading the strategies that produce them - built only from data that can be traced to an official source, with every unconfirmable value left blank and listed on the Flagged for review page.

Two tracks, one repo. A parallel session built a dashboard from the repo root (../index.html, generated from data/*.json by its own scripts/build_site.py). This board is the S1-b37bd750 track: CSV-first data, an arithmetic verification gate, and a model whose constants are fitted rather than assumed. Neither track overwrites the other's files, and both are published from the same Pages build. Cross-references are one click away in the left rail.

The board is generated. Every table you see is rendered from data/*.csv by scripts/build_board.py; if a value is not in the CSV it does not exist on the site, and if a CSV row fails an identity check the build stops. Start with the Executive summary, then review the Requirement readiness register for each requested capability, its blocker and the number or condition that would clear it. Use the Suggestion register to compare proposed paths before implementation.

What is real right now

  • 32 of 32 clubs loaded from the official standings page, identity-checked

(sum(PF) = sum(PA) = 6,291, sum(W) = sum(L) = 136).

  • 33 row-verified games of the 137 games the league has actually played through Week 9

(game results) - the rest is a coverage gap we declare, not a gap we paper over.

  • 160 availability rows for the Week 10 slate, read off the official injury report,

including the backup-vs-starter damage per position group — from 26 of the 28 clubs on the slate, because Baltimore and the Chargers publish no game status for the Monday fixture (FLG-028).

  • A priced Week 10 slate with fitted constants - see Lines. The market columns are

blank on purpose: no official source publishes odds, so nothing was filled in by hand.

  • 16 signals and 16 strategies, 13 validation gates, 20 open questions, 25 suggestions - each with a stated

dependency, so you can see what has to be bought or read before a decision is defensible.

The one-line status

The architecture, the data discipline and the evaluation machinery are done and reproducible. The pricing itself is not trustworthy yet, and it is not presented as such: the four numbers that make a spread into a price (home-field value, residual SD, shrinkage, vig) are fitted on 33 games and are labelled provisional-in-sample everywhere they appear.

Read it in this order

1. Executive summary - findings, not features. 2. Questions & gaps - what must be answered before any decision. 3. Suggestions - the paths, with impact/effort/risk; approve the ones you want. 4. Model → Lines → Signals → Correlation → Validation gates → Leaderboard & rewards. 5. Methodology and Verification log to audit the numbers, and Sources to click through to the official page behind any cell.

Nothing on this site is betting advice, and nothing here says a strategy is profitable. The best
claim the current data supports is: *this pipeline would detect it, and this is the evidence it
still needs.*

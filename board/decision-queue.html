Decision queue — 73 requirements, 7 decisions

The requirement register lists 73 requirements, each with the gate that blocks it and the number that would clear it. It is complete and it is not actionable: nobody reviews 73 rows and makes a call.

This page collapses it. Every requirement names a blocking gate; every gate is released by one owner decision. Grouping by gate produces seven decisions, and one of them releases a third of the register.

What that table is telling you

Q-02 releases 24 of 73 requirements and is a procurement decision, not a code decision. Every one of those 24 rows already has an official source registered. The blocker is not that we cannot find the data — it is that nobody has decided which of the available routes is authoritative: club pages verified club by club, an authorised structured feed, or publishing the field as unknown. That is one conversation.

Q-01 and G-03 release 10 rows between them and all 10 have no official source. They are the same decision counted twice: license a market price, or write down that no ROI, CLV or edge figure will be published. Both answers are defensible. What is not defensible is publishing a comparison against a price nobody licensed — which is why those columns are blank on every table on this site.

G-10 releases 15 rows and is the only purely engineering item. Scheduled capture with a clock time and an immutable store. This session proved the need for it rather than asserting it: the same official injury URL, read 48 hours apart, gave LAC a game status on 0 of 19 rows and then 9 of 19 (data freshness).

35 rows sit behind protocol decisions that cost nothing but resolve arguments. Sample window, fold dates, multiplicity rule, whether correlation may be measured on a priced market. These need no procurement and no code; they need someone to write them down and commit the text.

Q-04 (3 rows) and one row with no stated gate were not classified by this script. Their blocking gate is a coverage threshold rather than a single owner decision, so the queue says UNCLASSIFIED and lists the requirement IDs rather than inventing a decision for them.

Read the top of the queue in one minute

1. Decide the availability/depth source route (Q-02, 24 rows). Highest leverage, no cost. 2. Freeze the evaluation protocol (G-02/G-04/G-12/G-01/G-07, 35 rows). No cost, ends the "is this in-sample?" argument permanently. 3. Set the price-data policy (Q-01/G-03, 10 rows). Either license or accept the restriction in writing. 4. Stand up scheduled capture (G-10, 15 rows). The only item that needs engineering time. 5. Define the demotion state machine (G-11, 1 row). Small, but it is what makes a live strategy revertible.

Reproduce


python3 scripts/build_decisions_queue.py

It reads data/requirements_readiness.csv and changes nothing in it. Rows can sit behind more than one gate, so per-gate counts sum above 73 — that is stated in the output rather than adjusted away.

Every table on this site is generated from data/ by scripts/build_site.py; no value is typed into the HTML. Empty cells are deliberate: the value was not confirmable from an official source, so it is left blank and listed on the Flagged for review page. Nothing here is betting advice.

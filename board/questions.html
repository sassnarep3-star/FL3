Questions and information gaps

The brief asks for a clear list of questions, information and answers that must be addressed before any decision can reasonably be made, based on what we have and what we do not have. This is that list. It is a table rather than prose because it is a work queue: each row states what we already have, exactly what is missing, how to get it, and which decision is waiting on it. The final two columns are row-level provenance: source_id links to a registered official input where one applies; source_gap states what that source does not establish, or why no official source applies. A link is a place to verify an input, not proof that the question is answered. Open questions remain open until the missing evidence is captured and reconciled.

How to read the blocking column: yes = a decision that depends on this is currently undecidable with integrity (we would be guessing). no = it improves the model but does not gate the next step.

The seven that gate everything else

  • Q-01 market prices. We can build a number; we cannot yet say whether it is better than the

market's. Without opening and closing lines there is no CLV (gate G-03), no vig realism (G-06), and a leaderboard that ranks guesses. This is the only item on the list that is primarily a purchase.

  • Q-02 lineups. "Which backup is playing" is the heart of your brief. The league publishes a

designation, not a lineup (FLG-017), so every starter-quality adjustment in models/line_model.yaml is deliberately value: null.

  • Q-03 IR / transactions. A weekly report does not establish reserve status or a dated roster

history. The separate verified-source track has point-in-time report and roster checks, but this board still has no daily placement/activation series or general IR-duration rule. Treat individual examples as unresolved until their dated transaction chain is captured. Any absence from the report remains unknown, never "healthy".

  • Q-04 game-level history. The board model still consumes 33 game rows, but a separate official-source

verified corpus now contains 148 final games through Week 10 and reconciles 160 club W/L/T/PF/PA figures to standings. That corpus is not yet integrated into this board model, and no prior-season results corpus is present. Every fitted game distribution remains provisional; see the in-sample beta = 1.14 anomaly in FLG-026. Future 2026 games are not yet results and must not be backfilled.

  • Q-05/Q-06 calibration. The legacy in-sample fit on n = 33 gives beta = 1.14,

sigma_margin = 11.92 points and alpha = −0.82 points (SE 2.09). Separately, the verified track's 100-game walk-forward evaluation reports spread RMSE 12.58 versus 13.31 for a zero-margin baseline (gain +0.055, 95% CI −0.020 to +0.130) and Brier skill +0.069 versus a coin (95% CI −0.086 to +0.229). Both intervals include no improvement; the test is not a sportsbook comparison and does not validate the legacy fit parameters. A stable multi-season calibration and schedule adjustment remain open.

  • Q-07 correlation inputs. The board's eight provisional coefficients use two game-level pairs

(n = 33) and six team-level pairs (n = 32), each with an interval on the Correlation page. The separate 148-game corpus has not yet been used to recompute these outputs. Player-prop pairs and directly validated joint settlement probabilities remain unavailable, so G-07 still blocks combo ranking.

Questions we should not ask yet

These look like questions but are actually consequences of the above, and asking them now invites a made-up answer:

  • "Which signal is best?" - no signal has a settled sample (G-01).
  • "Is our model sharper than the market?" - undefined until Q-01.
  • "How much should we bet?" - Kelly on an unmeasured edge is just a faster way to be wrong (Q-17).
  • "Are parlays +EV here?" - needs the correlation measurements in Correlation,

which need Q-04 and Q-07.

Recommended next steps and owner-dependent gates

1. Continue only with official, permitted schedule/results and availability capture. Keep each read timestamped and immutable; distinguish the separate 148-game verified corpus from the board model's 33-row input, integrate through a checked change, and do not use unplayed games as outcomes. 2. Before any CLV/backtest publication, establish a licensed price-data source, permitted retention, and settlement keys. Until that exists, leave market values blank and keep the price gate blocked. 3. Keep all strategies in shadow/paper status. A 100-game walk-forward evaluation exists in the separate verified track, but its uncertainty includes no improvement and it is not a market comparison. No stake, reward or live-demotion policy is inferred from a model result. 4. Treat legal scope, price licensing, infrastructure credentials and bankroll authority as explicit owner-dependent questions. The site records the missing evidence and best next path; it cannot supply contracts, legal approval or account access on the owner's behalf. 5. Compare implementation options on the Suggestions page before changing the system. Record a choice and rationale there; only then should a path that depends on that choice be implemented.

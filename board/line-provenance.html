Our line — three files, three answers

Read this before trusting any number on the lines page.

The repository publishes "our line" for the same Week 10 fixtures from three different files, and they do not agree. This is not a rounding dispute. On the same game they can be seven points apart, on opposite sides of the number, and both are presented to a reader as our own forecast.

Three files, three key formats, no generator overlap. That is why this went unnoticed: no single script ever loaded two of them at once.

The convention trap

scripts/compute_lines.py emits home_spread = round(-margin, 1). scripts/model.py emits expected_home_margin with the opposite sign convention. So the two files are on inverted axes and different levels. Anyone comparing them naively, or joining them by club name, gets a number that is wrong twice over.

Every value on this page is converted to one axis: signed home margin, positive = home wins by that many points.

Three things to take from this table, and one thing not to.

What it shows:

1. A and B disagree with each other by more than either disagrees with reality. Mean absolute disagreement between our own two files is 6.72 points. A model whose internal disagreement exceeds its external error is not one model with two outputs — it is two models. 2. The live root site renders the worst of the three. A's MAE is 11.62 against B's 8.02. The file a public reader actually sees is the one that leans hardest toward home teams, with a bias of +7.84 points. 3. None of them beats a naive pick-em by much. Always forecasting a zero home margin scores 13.00 MAE. B scores 8.02, which is better; A scores 11.62, which is close enough to the do-nothing benchmark that its apparent sophistication is not buying much.

What it does not show — and this is important:

n = 12. That is a provenance audit, not a model evaluation. Ranking A against B at n=12 is not statistically meaningful and this page does not do it. B being closer on twelve games is a reason to investigate B's convention, not a reason to promote B. Any promotion has to go through the gates in data/strategies.json, and one 12-game sample clears none of them.

Why this is the most serious finding in the repository

Every other defect found this session is a gap — missing data, missing pages, missing timestamps. A gap is honest: the cell is blank and the reader knows. This one is different. Two files with cannot-both-be-true values are both published, both labelled as ours, on two different pages of the same site, with no reconciliation anywhere. A reader who visits the root dashboard and then the board gets two different answers to "what did we think the line was", and nothing tells them that.

What is being done about it, and what is not

Done this session: the three files are reconciled on one axis, the disagreement is published here and as data/line_provenance_reconciliation.csv, and the convention difference is documented in code.

Not done, deliberately: no file was rewritten, no model was promoted, and the root site was not switched to B. Choosing a canonical series is a modelling decision with gate consequences. It gets a register row and an owner decision, not a silent swap by a session that also wrote the comparison.

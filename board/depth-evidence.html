Depth and replacement evidence — what the club pages actually publish

The brief asks the system to track "2nd, 3rd or 4th string backups having to replace starters." This page records what could be verified about that from the clubs' own pages on 2026-11-16, and — just as importantly — what those pages cannot be made to say.

Every claim below was checked against the live page. Where the brief's description did not match what the page publishes, the page wins and the discrepancy is recorded.

Six distinct structures break a naive parse, and all six are present in just three clubs:

  • shared_slot — two players in one cell. Seattle WR. Whoever reads column 1 as "the WR1"

invents a starter the club did not name.

  • duplicate_rank1 — one player at rank 1 of two positions. Green Bay publishes **Kitan

Oladapo** at rank 1 of both safety spots. That is one player covering two slots, not two starters. Reading it as two starters would double-count him in any availability calculation.

  • duplicate_rank2 — one backup, two roles. Baltimore's Ryan Neuzil is rank 2 at both LG

and C; Carson Vinson is rank 2 at both LT and RT. Losing one starter promotes the same man.

  • empty_rank1 — Baltimore publishes no kicker at all at rank 1.
  • continuation_row — Green Bay's RB4 Kaleb Johnson sits under a blank position cell,

continuing the row above. A parser keyed on the position column silently drops him.

  • image_only — Houston. The data is not in the document at all.

Two of three charts carry no date whatsoever. A chart with no date cannot be versioned, cannot be proved pre-kickoff, and cannot enter a backtest without lookahead risk. This is the concrete reason the brief's "official depth-chart register with date and source per row" requirement cannot be met from club pages alone: the clubs do not publish the date.

This is a real, sourced, reproducible feature — and it is the one place this repository can honestly say it detects "a backup replacing a starter" rather than inferring it.

And here is what it does not unlock

Depth rank is not talent. The chart tells us who is next. It says nothing about how much worse next is. Every availability constant in this repository (QB 2.6, WR 0.55, CB 0.5, …, in data/lines_week10.json) is an assumption dressed as a parameter. The brief's own requirement is the correct one: replace those constants with an ablation — run the identical model with and without the availability features and report the difference with uncertainty. That cannot be done until there are enough settled games to measure it, and there is exactly one week.

One cautionary observation, offered as an observation

In the two games where these charts can be checked against a result:

  • Seattle had its starting quarterback, left tackle, left guard and top defensive tackle

all Out — and won 21-13.

  • Green Bay had its starting running back, centre and a starting linebacker Out — and lost

30-10.

That is one favourable and one unfavourable case, which is another way of saying it is evidence of nothing. It is recorded here because it is a useful antidote to the intuition that a long injury list must move a line, and because a future ablation will need exactly this kind of pairing.

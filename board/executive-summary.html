The one-paragraph version

The repository holds a real, source-linked NFL dataset and a working origination engine, and as of today it can be scored for the first time. The score is: not established. Over 11 pre-kickoff forecasts the model beat a coin flip on the Brier score (+0.194) with an interval that includes zero ([-0.018, +0.385]), lost to the far stronger benchmark of always quoting the sample's own base rate (-0.016), and won 1.4 more games than its own probabilities said it should. Its opinions are also four times too narrow to beat a market. Separately, and more seriously, the repository was publishing three different numbers called "our line" for the same fixtures, on two opposite sign conventions, and the site's data snapshot predated the results of the games it prices. Both of those are now measured and published rather than inherited.

A provenance caveat that must be repeated wherever these captures are cited. The hash in data/captures/manifest.json covers the transcription, not NFL.com's raw HTTP bytes, because the agent's fetch tool returns extracted text rather than bytes. It makes the capture tamper-evident and re-checkable row by row; it is not a hash of the source document and must not be described as one.

2. The four findings that change decisions

2.1 The model is not yet distinguishable from luck, and it is worse than a trivial benchmark

TestResultReading
Brier skill vs coin flip+0.194, 95% CI [-0.018, +0.385]interval includes zero
Brier skill vs sample base rate-0.016worse than always quoting "27% home"
Picks won vs expected8 / 11 vs 6.61 expected+1.39 wins of luck
Dispersion ratio (model sd ÷ realised sd)0.24opinions ~4× too narrow

This is not a verdict that the model is bad. It is a verdict that 11 games cannot say, and the honest presentation of "we cannot say" is the interval, not a headline. What is actionable is the dispersion ratio: a forecast that barely varies across games cannot produce an edge over a market no matter how good its average is, and no amount of re-centring fixes that.

2.2 Three files publish different numbers called "our line"

data/lines_week10.json (rendered on the public root site), data/lines_origination.csv (rendered on the board) and data/2026-week10-lines.csv (which no generator writes) each publish a Week 10 line. They use two opposite sign conventions and disagree by 6.72 points on average on the same fixtures — more than either disagrees with reality. On WAS-NYG, decided by 3 points, one file was ~7 points wrong one way and another ~6.5 points wrong the other way.

Nothing was rewritten. Choosing a canonical series is a decision with gate consequences and belongs to the owner, not to a session that also wrote the comparison. The reconciliation is published so the decision can be made on evidence.

2.3 The snapshot predates the results it prices

Pages are stamped 2026-11-14; the Sunday slate was played 2026-11-15. Re-capture shows 22 of 32 clubs changed and the injury report going from 160 rows / 26 clubs to 347 rows / 28 clubs.

The sharpest form of this is a controlled comparison the repository accidentally made possible: two independent transcriptions of the same official URL, 48 hours apart. They disagree substantively on 16 rows. LAC carried a game status on 0 of 19 rows on 2026-11-14 and 9 of 19 on 2026-11-16 — correct both times, because a Monday club has not had its final practice yet. The brief's demand to treat the injury report as a timestamped series, not a weekly snapshot is therefore not housekeeping. Pricing from a frozen read uses less information than was available and presents it as the state of the world.

2.4 Depth charts cannot be read as "column 1 = the starter"

Confirmed against the live club pages: Houston publishes its chart as a raster image; Green Bay prepends jersey numbers; Seattle puts two players in one cell; Baltimore publishes no kicker; Green Bay has one player at rank 1 of both safety spots; Baltimore has one backup listed at rank 2 of two positions. Three of these produce a fabricated starter, a doubled player, or a dropped player under a naive parse.

What the data does support is the feature the brief actually asked for: 8 rank-1 players were also Out or Doubtful on the official report, each with the club's named replacement and that replacement's own designation — replacement depth, not merely replacement presence.

5. What to decide first

1. Name a canonical line series and a single sign convention. Until then two pages of this site give different answers to "what did we think". 2. Set the price-data policy. Either license a feed or accept, in writing, that no ROI, CLV or edge number will be published. Both are defensible; publishing an unlicensed comparison is not. 3. Decide the depth-source route for Houston (and any other image-only club): authorised feed, or publish the field as unknown. Never transcribe by eye. 4. Accept or reject the dispersion finding as the top modelling priority. It is the only finding here that no amount of additional data collection fixes by itself.

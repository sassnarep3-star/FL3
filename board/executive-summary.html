Executive summary

Read: 2026-11-14. Season state: Week 10, Thursday game played, 13 Sunday games and one Monday game still to come. Repo state at start: one 5-byte README.md, one commit, no code, no data (FLG-001).

1. What this project now is

A data-to-price pipeline with a review discipline attached:

ingest → verify → derive → rate → price → signal → gate → board, where nothing enters the site that does not carry a source id, and nothing is priced off an unverified input.

  • 19 sources registered in sources.csv; 9 read this session from the league's own

pages (standings, injury report, three team-stat pages, two schedule pages, scores page, the 2026 rulebook page), 1 snippet-level, 9 registered-but-unread because they are purchase or follow-up items.

  • 18 checks in the verification log: 14 pass, 1 warning (rounding boundaries),

1 fail that does not block the build but does block trust (V-09b, the league's own tables disagree), plus coverage and integrity rows. A blocking failure aborts the build.

  • 23 data tables in this track: 9 read from official pages, 5 registers authored from the brief

(questions, suggestions, signals, gates, flags), 9 generated by the pipeline; 4 model/spec YAML files and 8 scripts, all reproducible with one command (python3 scripts/build_board.py).

3. The uncomfortable findings (these are the useful ones)

1. The injury report alone cannot do the job you asked for. It lists designations, not lineups. It omits players on IR entirely: the Week 10 Baltimore report contains 8 rows and no game status, while the league's own news feed on the same page says the franchise QB is out 6-10 weeks (FLG-005). Any "defence is down to its 4th-string CB" logic must merge the report with transactions, inactive lists and club depth charts - and no league-wide depth chart exists (FLG-017). 2. Stat pages on nfl.com publish numbers with no machine-readable column labels. We could only publish 28 of 39 columns; the rest stayed blank because a magnitude is not a label (FLG-014). The columns we did publish are pinned by identities (league pass attempts on offense = league pass attempts on defense = 9,037, etc.), which is why the mapping is provable rather than plausible. 3. The two official tables disagree with each other about scoring events: offense pass TD = 414 vs defense TD allowed = 415, INT thrown = 202 vs INT forced = 200 (FLG-025). Those four columns are published but marked pricing-ineligible. A model that silently used them would inherit a bug it could never see. 4. There is no official source for odds. No NFL-controlled page publishes a spread, total, moneyline or prop price (FLG-010). So every "market" column on this site is blank. Originating lines is now possible; scoring them against the market is a purchase decision. 5. The first model we built is a good example of a bad line. Unshrunk net-points ratings produce things like "JAX -24 at TEN", which is nonsense. Fitting the conversion on our 33 verified games even returns beta = 1.14 - i.e. anti-shrinkage, and a total-model exponent pinned on the edge of its search grid (FLG-026). Those two facts are the clearest available evidence that the sample is too small to calibrate anything, which is exactly what the gates are for. 6. A handful of Week 10 rows list players on clubs they have not been associated with before 2026 (Garrett on LA, Evans on SF, Marvin Harrison Jr. on HOU, Nelson on SEA, McCarthy on NYG…). Each is either a real 2026 transaction or a feed error; we did not assume either way and put them all in a queue for an affiliation audit (FLG-006).

4. What has to be answered before a decision

20 questions in Questions & gaps; 7 are blocking: market prices (Q-01), lineups (Q-02), IR/transactions (Q-03), game-level history (Q-04), the calibration constants (Q-05/Q-06) and the correlation inputs (Q-07). Each one names what we have, what we lack, exactly how to obtain it, and which decision it unblocks.

6. What is deliberately not here

No bet results, no ROI, no "record" of any strategy, no odds values, no weather numbers, no snap counts, no player-level prop rates. Each of those absences is a row on Flagged for review or a question in Questions & gaps, because inventing them is the failure mode this whole repo is organised to prevent.

Every table on this site is generated from data/ by scripts/build_site.py; no value is typed into the HTML. Empty cells are deliberate: the value was not confirmable from an official source, so it is left blank and listed on the Flagged for review page. Nothing here is betting advice.

Who was supposed to play, who actually did, and how deep the replacement had to go

The "expected" side is the declared depth order. The "actual" side is the official Game Book — the only NFL document that states a starting lineup. 12 of the 14 Week 10 games have a published Game Book and all 12 were transcribed. The two games without one (PIT@CIN, LAC@BAL) are absent from the graph rather than assumed.

The finding that changes how injuries should be priced

The official report's game status is a level. What it predicts about whether a player actually started is a separate question, and the Game Books answer it. This table is the empirical answer on the only week where both documents exist.

Read it carefully. "Not observable" is not "did nothing". A Game Book lists starters, substitutions, did-not-play and not-active; this capture transcribed three of those four blocks reliably, so a player who is in none of them cannot be placed and is reported as unobservable. That is why the denominators differ, and why the column is printed rather than quietly dropped.

The practical consequence: a "Questionable" depth-1 player started 84% of the time in this week (16 of 19 observed), while a player with no designation at all started 98% of the time. The designation separates a little; it does not separate nearly as much as a naive penalty would assume, and "Out" is the only category that reliably predicts absence.

Every replacement came from depth 2 or 3 — no club in this week reached deeper than its third string at a position where the starter was inactive. That is a real constraint on any model that wants to charge a large penalty for a "depth" problem: at this point in the season the replacements are, in the main, the next listed man.

What this graph deliberately does not do

  • It does not read the depth order off a jersey number, a salary, or draft position. The order comes from a declared third-party depth source, and the file records that source on every row.
  • It does not turn a missing name into a benching. Absence from a transcribed block yields "not listed", never "did not play".
  • It does not credit a replacement as a starter unless the Game Book's own lineup names him there.
  • It does not claim "Out" means "did not play" — the table above tests exactly that, and finds it holds for the Week 10 sample without being a law.

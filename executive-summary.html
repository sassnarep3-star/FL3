Executive summary

One page: what this project can now say, what it cannot, and the smallest set of decisions that would change that. Written 2026-11-17 against the 2026 regular season through Week 10, plus the published Week 11 schedule.

The one-paragraph version. The repository can now originate a line four different ways on the same clock and score all four against the same controls, on 148 verified games that reconcile to the league's own standings for all 32 clubs. Every strategy beats a coin flip on the win-probability score and three of four beat a pick'em on spread error, but not one of them survives the multiple-comparison threshold (0 of 4 cleared), the totals are worse than a control that ignores the teams entirely, and the model's opinions are still only a quarter to a half as wide as reality. So the honest position is: the machinery works, the signal is unproven, and no price exists to turn a probability into money. Everything below is the detail behind that paragraph.

1. What is established

  • A verified results corpus. 148 games through Week 10, identity-checked against the official standings: wins, losses, ties, points for and points against match for all 32 clubs, and the league total balances at 6,824 points each way. Source: nfl.com league standings and the per-week schedule pages.
  • A leakage-free harness. Every forecast for week w is built only from weeks before w. The code enforces it structurally, not by discipline: the week's results are added to the model's state only after that week's forecasts are produced.
  • Four genuinely different origination strategies plus four controls, scored on proper scores and on pricing error, with game-cluster intervals and a published multiplicity adjustment over 12 comparisons (α = 0.0042).
  • The best spread error in this repo so far is 10.03 points MAE (S2) against 10.66 for a pick'em control — a gain of 0.63 points, with an interval that overlaps zero.
  • Measured correlations with n, published as numbers rather than assumptions, because that is the only way a parlay gate can be honest.
  • A prospective Week 11 slate frozen before kickoff, with the market columns blank.

2. What is not established — and will not be claimed

  • Skill. The best strategy's paired gain against pick'em is z = -2.43; the threshold for 12 comparisons is 2.87. Nothing is ranked as skilful anywhere on this site.
  • Totals. The best strategy's total error is 11.55 against 11.36 for a trailing-league-mean control, and the model's predicted total correlates with the actual total at r = 0.02. The model cannot price a total.
  • Any money metric. There is no licensed price in the repo, so ROI, CLV and edge are not computed, not estimated and not implied. The staking column of every leaderboard is deliberately empty.
  • Depth and availability. No official source publishes a depth ordinal, so "second string" cannot be established. The repo says so rather than inferring it.
  • A complete point-in-time record. The scheduled availability capture has never completed a successful run, so no pre-kickoff series exists for any week.

4. What was done in this session

  • Built the comparison lab (scripts/build_compare.py): four strategies, four controls, walk-forward, coupled-cluster bootstrap, Bonferroni ledger, calibration, luck panel, regime split, three-way total comparison, and a published per-game ledger.
  • Published two new pages: this summary and Compare strategies, with 16 decision questions and 10 suggestions, each carrying selectable paths and a recommendation, so a path can be chosen before anything is implemented.
  • Checked whether the availability capture works, and found a defect in the alarm rather than in the data. The scheduled capture's only run (2026-11-16T22:54Z) failed with exit code 2. The cause was that the Week 11 injury route still renders the final Week 10 tables before the league publishes the week's report, and the drift check treated that as corruption. The check now classifies not published yet separately from drift, records it as a dated line in snapshots/index.csv, stores no body, and is unit-tested on synthetic pages in the workflow. A page that serves a later week than requested is still treated as drift and still fails the build.
  • Asserted nothing new about the league. The Week 11 official injury report is not published yet, so no Week 11 availability is used anywhere; the Week 11 schedule was re-read from the official page and is used only for the prospective slate. The fixture page for IND-HOU was also read for its venue, which is outside the lab's scope and is not used as a model input.
  • Changed no existing dataset. The 148-game corpus, the standings snapshot and the Week 10 captures are inputs here, read-only. Where this lab's numbers differ from an older page, both are published and the difference is on the Flagged for Review page.

5. The arithmetic of "how much longer"

The intervals on this page are about 2.5–3.0 points of MAE wide on 148 games. The strategies differ from each other by less than 0.6 points and from the pick'em control by less than 1.0. For a paired difference to clear even an unadjusted 95% threshold at the observed effect size, the sample has to grow roughly by the square of the ratio of the two — a factor of about five to seven, i.e. 700–900 games, or three seasons. For the Bonferroni-adjusted threshold used here, more. No amount of cleverness on 148 games substitutes for that, which is why the brief's own insistence on pre-registration and honest intervals is the right call.
